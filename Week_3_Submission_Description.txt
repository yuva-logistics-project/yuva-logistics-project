This report explores the simulated logistics dataset using descriptive statistics, group comparisons, correlation analysis, and visualizations generated with Python. It outlines the dataset variables and examines delivery duration, shipment distance, parcel weight, transport cost, service priority, traffic, weather, vehicle type, and delivery zone. The report includes visual outputs for delivery-time distribution, average delivery time by zone, the relationship between distance and delivery duration, and average cost by vehicle type. Each chart is selected to answer a practical question: a histogram shows spread and skew, a bar chart compares operational groups, a scatter plot reveals relationships and potential exceptions, and summary statistics quantify central tendency and variability. The accompanying code uses pandas and matplotlib so the analysis can be rerun and audited. Interpretations are framed as patterns to investigate rather than proven causal claims, because the records are simulated and the results cannot establish real-world performance. The report explains how managers could use these views to identify zones for further investigation, check whether long-distance shipments take longer, and compare costs while accounting for vehicle capacity and service requirements. It ends with recommendations for validating these hypotheses on real operational data before making policy or routing changes. The visualizations are accompanied by explanations of what each chart can and cannot establish, helping prevent overconfident conclusions from small or synthetic samples. The report encourages checking group sizes, validating unexpected points, and combining charts with domain knowledge before recommending changes. It also discusses how the same reporting structure could be reused in a real logistics team through regular KPI reviews and documented comparisons over time.
