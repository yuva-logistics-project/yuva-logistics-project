This report presents the strategic planning stage of a simulated logistics data analytics project focused on delivery-time performance and transport cost. The scenario describes a regional parcel distribution company that wants to understand delays, compare delivery zones and vehicle types, and improve capacity planning. The report defines the business problem, scope, assumptions, measurable objectives, and key performance indicators, including average delivery time, on-time delivery rate, average transport cost per shipment, and cost per kilometre. It also explains how descriptive statistics, exploratory data analysis, regression, clustering, and optimization can support logistics decisions. A staged roadmap is provided from data sourcing and validation through cleaning, feature engineering, visualization, predictive modelling, evaluation, and recommendations. Python snippets illustrate how shipment records could be loaded and summarized with pandas and how the analysis could be structured reproducibly. The project uses simulated records for demonstration and clearly distinguishes planned methods from verified business findings. The expected benefit is a practical framework that helps logistics managers identify operational bottlenecks, monitor service levels, and test evidence-based improvements before implementing them in a real operation. The report also identifies assumptions, risks, ethical considerations, and the evidence that would be needed before using the approach in a live business. It recommends documenting KPI definitions, retaining raw data, recording transformation decisions, and presenting findings in a way that operational staff can interpret. The roadmap is designed to be feasible for a student project and can be extended later as more reliable data becomes available.
